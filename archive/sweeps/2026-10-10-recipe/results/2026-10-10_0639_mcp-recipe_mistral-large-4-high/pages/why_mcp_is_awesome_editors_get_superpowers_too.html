Why MCP Is Awesome: Editors Get Superpowers Too

MCP is not just a developer's toy. When a CMS speaks MCP, content editors can model schema, manage media and publish pages from the tools they already love.

Developers were the first to feel the magic of MCP, but content editors are quietly getting the biggest upgrade. When a CMS exposes itself over MCP, the backoffice stops being the only place where content work happens.

Imagine an editor sitting in their favourite writing tool, asking an assistant to draft a page, pick images from the media library, translate the copy and hit publish. The assistant calls the same MCP tools a human would click through — with the same permissions, the same validation and the same audit trail.

That is the superpower: editors keep their workflow, and the CMS keeps its rules. Nobody has to learn a new interface, because the interface they already use now understands the CMS.

The front page you are reading was assembled by an MCP-driven pipeline: a block grid of rich text, images and quotes, published under a domain, rendered by Razor views that follow Umbraco's own conventions. Every step — schema, media, content, publishing — happened through tools, not through hand-edited files.

For teams, that means reviewable, repeatable, scriptable content operations. A new site? Model it once over MCP and every environment can be rebuilt from the same calls. A new post every week? The assistant does the typing; the editor does the judging.

MCP is awesome because it turns the CMS from a walled garden into a well-lit workshop — and hands the editors the keys.

The best interface is the one you already have.
