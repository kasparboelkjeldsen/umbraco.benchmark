One Protocol, Every Assistant

8 October 2026

Plenty of standards fail because they are clever. MCP is succeeding because it is boring: JSON-RPC messages over stdio or HTTP, a small surface of tools, resources and prompts, and versioned capabilities negotiated at startup. Any language, any runtime, any assistant.

Boring is what lets an ecosystem compound. A server written for one client works on all of them; a client improvement benefits every server at once.

The compounding is already visible: databases, design tools, browsers and content systems — including this Umbraco site — expose themselves over MCP, and each new server raises the value of every client. No N×M integration matrix, just one protocol each side speaks.

When you evaluate your next stack, ask one question: can it speak MCP? If yes, it is not just a tool, it is part of the graph.

“A good protocol disappears. MCP's greatest success will be that nobody talks about it — they just use it.”

— The Umbraco.Bench blog
