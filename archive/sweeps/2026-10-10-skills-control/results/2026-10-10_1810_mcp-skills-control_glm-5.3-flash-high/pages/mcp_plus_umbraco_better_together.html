MCP + Umbraco: better together

Two servers, one backoffice — how the schema/content split makes agentic CMS work clean.

<p>This site runs on <strong>two</strong> MCP servers. The <em>umbraco</em> server owns the schema: document types, element types, data types, templates, media types, domains. The <em>umbraco-editor</em> server owns the content: pages, blocks, publishing. One is the architect; the other is the editor.</p><p>The split matters because it mirrors how a real team works. Schema changes are deliberate and rare; content changes are constant and safe. Putting them behind separate tool surfaces keeps the risky operations visible and the routine ones frictionless.</p>

“Schema tools for the architect, content tools for the editor — and neither has to pretend to be the other.”

— Two servers, one backoffice
<p>And when the two meet in the middle — a block grid configured by one server, filled with content by the other — you get exactly what you're reading: a site whose every layer was built over MCP, verified by reading back what actually landed.</p>
