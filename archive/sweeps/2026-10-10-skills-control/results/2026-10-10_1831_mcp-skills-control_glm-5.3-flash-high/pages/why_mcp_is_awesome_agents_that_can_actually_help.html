Why MCP is awesome: agents that can actually help

An AI assistant is only as useful as the things it can reach. MCP gives agents a safe, standard way to reach your CMS — this very post is proof.

An AI assistant is only as useful as the things it can actually reach. A brilliant writing partner that cannot see the content tree, the media library or the publish button is a partner locked out of the building. MCP changes that: it gives agents a standard, permissioned way to work with real systems — including a CMS like Umbraco.

Photo: Edge2Edge Media / Unsplash

With an MCP server, the assistant does not have to guess what an article is or how a hero is put together. The content model itself tells it: here are the types, here are the fields, here is what publishes. The agent works the way an editor works — creating drafts, filling in blocks, and asking before anything goes live.

This post is the demo

There is a neat bit of recursion here: this page, the hero on the front page, and the images in both posts were all created through Umbraco's MCP server. No bespoke script, no hand-poked database — just a protocol conversation. The assistant proposed the schema, the user confirmed each action, and the result is a site that was modeled and filled in minutes.

That is the promise of MCP: not magic, just a common language. And once everything speaks it, the interesting question stops being can the agent reach the CMS, and becomes what should we ask it to do next.
