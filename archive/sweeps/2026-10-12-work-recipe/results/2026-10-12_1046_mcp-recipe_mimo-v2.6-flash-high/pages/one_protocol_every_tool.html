One Protocol, Every Tool

8 October 2026

Before MCP every integration was a one-off. Now a server you write on Monday works with every assistant on Tuesday. A look at what that means for day-to-day work.

The promise of MCP is unglamorous and that is exactly why it works: a server exposes what it can do, a client asks for it, and both sides agree on the shape of the conversation. No plugin marketplaces, no vendor handshake, no per-assistant packaging.

We noticed the change first in review meetings. Instead of arguing about which assistant to buy, we were writing small servers — an afternoon each — and letting every assistant in the company use them.

The numbers surprised us. Our first server — read-only access to the CMS content store — took less than a day including tests. The second took an hour, because the hard decisions had already been made once.

That is the compounding part. The protocol is the same every time, so the learning lives in your team instead of in each integration.

We also got a security story we could actually explain. Every server declares its tools, every client declares its scopes, and the whole exchange is visible in one log. Nothing hides inside a prompt.

Compare that with the alternative, where the only way to know what an assistant can reach is to ask it and hope the answer is honest.

One contract, written down once, and every assistant we have ever tried just works with it.

— Tomás, engineering manager
