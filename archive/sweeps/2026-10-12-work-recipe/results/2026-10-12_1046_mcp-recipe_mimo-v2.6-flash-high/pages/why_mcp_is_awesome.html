Why MCP Is Awesome

1 October 2026

One protocol, and suddenly every tool in your stack speaks the same language. Here is why the Model Context Protocol changed how we wire assistants to real systems.

The first time an assistant reached into our issue tracker without anyone writing a line of glue code, the room went quiet. That is the moment MCP stops being a specification and starts being infrastructure.

Before MCP, every tool meant a new integration: a new auth flow, a new schema, a new set of prompts explaining how the thing worked. Multiply that by the number of tools a team actually uses and you get an impossible maintenance bill. MCP collapses all of it into one contract.

Three things make it stick. Portability — a server written for one assistant works with the next one that walks through the door. Scope — you decide exactly which tools a given client may touch, so the scary ones stay behind a permission. And speed of delivery — a useful server is an afternoon, not a sprint.

None of that is magic. It is the payoff for choosing a boring, well-specified protocol over a clever proprietary one.

The part people underestimate is the blast radius on the boring work. Status pings, draft posts, changelog entries, the weekly report — all of it becomes a call instead of a chore, and all of it lands in the same audit trail.

Once the tools are on the protocol, the interesting question stops being "can the assistant do this?" and starts being "should it?". That is a much better conversation to be having.

We stopped budgeting for integrations and started budgeting for ideas.

— Priya, platform lead
